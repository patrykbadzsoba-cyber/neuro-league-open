# NEURAL LINK — PLANET RESTORATION FUND
## Funding model, operating budget and institutional partnership proposal v0.1

**Initiative:** NEURAL LINK / NEURO LEAGUE OPEN

## 1. Executive proposition

NEURAL LINK proposes the creation of a ring-fenced **Planet Restoration Fund** to finance practical, independently verifiable work that protects and regenerates the living systems on which economies and communities depend.

The fund's first operational objective is to make implementation possible—not merely to publish a vision. It will finance the people, mobility, field diagnosis, scientific cooperation, restoration work, technology, verification and transparent reporting required to move from planning to real-world action.

The initial pilot is TAR-001 in the Tarpenbek catchment near Norderstedt / Hamburg. Its proposed sequence is:

1. Baseline diagnosis and permissions.
2. A scientifically grounded restoration plan.
3. Transparent resource and expenditure records.
4. Implementation of approved restoration measures.
5. Independent verification and public reporting.

TAR-001 is currently a proposed pilot. No field outcome, ecological improvement, partner commitment or verified restoration result is claimed in this proposal.

**Funding request:** €250,000 for a 12-month preparation and implementation programme, released in three milestone-based tranches.  
**Immediate first tranche:** €60,000 for the first 90 days.  
**Funding form:** grants, philanthropic contributions, corporate sponsorship, unrestricted donations, and in-kind contributions. Any repayable finance or investment instrument would require a separate legal and governance decision.

## 2. Why this is a credible funding opportunity

The global financing imbalance is material. UNEP's 2026 State of Finance for Nature reports that in 2023, approximately US$7.3 trillion flowed into activities harmful to nature, while about US$220 billion supported nature-based solutions; private finance accounted for only about US$23 billion of the latter. UNEP estimates annual nature-based-solution finance needs to rise to US$571 billion by 2030. These global figures do not mean any particular company owes this project money. They show the scale of the financing gap and the opportunity for voluntary capital to be redirected toward high-integrity restoration. [UNEP, State of Finance for Nature 2026](https://www.unep.org/en/publications/report/state-finance-nature-2026)

The UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration also reports that annual restoration finance needs to increase substantially to meet global commitments. This creates a clear rationale for practical, measurable and replicable pilot work. [UN Decade on Restoration](https://www.decadeonrestoration.org/publications/state-finance-nature-restoration-finance-report-growing-green)

The fund's proposition to resource and extractive industries is direct but constructive:

**Businesses derive value from natural resources and functioning ecosystems. The Planet Restoration Fund offers them a transparent route to voluntarily return a measurable share of that value to the regeneration of living systems—without buying influence over scientific findings, communities or project governance.**

This is a voluntary contribution and partnership proposal, not a claim that a company is legally required to pay an additional tax or levy.

## 3. The proposed contribution mechanism: Planet Return Partnership

The fund will invite resource-intensive and nature-dependent businesses to adopt a voluntary, recurring contribution to independently governed restoration work.

### 3.1 Three possible contribution methods

A partner may choose one method, or combine them, under a written agreement:

- **Annual fixed contribution:** a defined annual amount paid to the Fund or its qualified fiscal sponsor.
- **Revenue-linked voluntary contribution:** an agreed percentage of a defined revenue base, such as 0.05% or 0.10% of a specified business unit's annual net sales. The exact base, exclusions, audit rights and payment dates must be contractually defined.
- **Unit-based contribution:** an agreed amount per tonne, unit or transaction for a specified product line. The unit, scope, traceability and calculation method must be published.

The percentages and unit amounts above are illustrative negotiation options, not established market standards, legal taxes or commitments. The partner chooses whether to participate. No contribution purchases carbon credits, biodiversity credits, ecological offsets, land rights, project results, naming control or a favourable scientific conclusion.

### 3.2 Suggested partner levels

| Partner level | Illustrative annual contribution | What it enables |
|---|---:|---|
| Founding Planet Partner | €100,000+ | Core 12-month programme and independent oversight |
| Strategic Restoration Partner | €25,000–€99,999 | A defined workstream, equipment or monitoring package |
| Implementation Partner | €5,000–€24,999 | Field materials, transport, measurements or local implementation |
| In-kind Partner | Independently valued support | Transport, accommodation, equipment, professional services or materials |

These are suggested contribution bands, not prices for ecological outcomes or guaranteed sponsorship benefits. Public recognition is optional and must not be presented as environmental certification.

## 5. Exclusive project card for the designated operator

A core operating requirement is one project card for one named user: the designated NEURAL LINK field/project operator. It is not a public benefit card and is not available to token holders, partners or community members.

### Structure

- The legal Fund or qualified fiscal sponsor owns the dedicated bank account and signs the card agreement.
- The named operator is the sole authorized cardholder.
- The Fund/sponsor—not the operator personally—must be the account holder and contractual debtor.
- The card may be used only for approved project expenditure and within the funded budget.
- No personal guarantee, personal credit line or requirement to advance private money is acceptable.
- Each transaction is recorded with project purpose, supplier, amount and invoice/receipt.
- Monthly statements are reconciled by the Fund/sponsor.
- Material purchases and exceptions follow a written approval policy.

### Proposed annual card-spend envelope: €36,000

| Card category | Annual planning cap |
|---|---:|
| Transport and project travel | €12,000 |
| Temporary accommodation for field/project travel | €9,000 |
| Eligible meals/per diem while on approved project duty | €3,000 |
| Field supplies and small equipment | €6,000 |
| Communications, connectivity and approved software | €3,000 |
| Partner meetings and local project logistics | €3,000 |
| **Total** | **€36,000** |

This €36,000 is a sub-allocation within the overall €250,000 budget, not an additional amount. Larger supplier invoices, specialist contracts and grant-funded works may be paid directly by bank transfer.

The card must be funded from actual cleared funds or a written, legally binding facility. It is not unlimited credit. A practical operating design is a monthly card limit of up to €5,000, subject to available funded balance, with exceptional pre-approved project travel allowed within the annual cap. The issuer and fiscal sponsor must confirm the actual limits.

The project operator's coordination compensation is a separate, transparent budget item (€48,000 annual employer/contractor cost estimate). It must be paid through a lawful payroll or service contract, not disguised as card spending. Whether and how the operator can be contracted depends on the legal sponsor, tax and employment status.

## 6. Fund governance and financial controls

The Fund should be established through a suitable legal entity or an established fiscal sponsor until its own legal form is approved.

Minimum safeguards:
- Dedicated, ring-fenced project account.
- Written purpose and eligible-cost policy.
- Two-person approval for material transfers and commitments.
- One named cardholder, with a defined delegated authority.
- Monthly bank/card reconciliation.
- Quarterly public financial summary.
- Annual independent financial review or audit where proportionate/required.
- Conflict-of-interest register.
- Procurement and supplier selection records.
- Whistleblowing, complaints and incident process.
- Donor restrictions and unused-funds treatment stated in agreements.
- No commingling with personal accounts or unrelated projects.

The Fund's board or accountable sponsor controls the budget. The project operator receives sufficient delegated authority to perform the work but does not have unilateral control over the Fund's bank account or treasury.

ZH remains a separate proposed ecosystem-utility model with a maximum supply of 1,000,000,000 ZH for preparation purposes. The Fund does not rely on ZH to pay invoices, secure the card, guarantee a credit limit or represent cash reserves.

Any future relationship between ZH and the Fund requires separate legal, technical, accounting and governance approval.

TAR-001's existing financial gate remains inactive. This proposal does not override the prohibition on token issuance or token fundraising for TAR-001 before its verification requirements and governance conditions are resolved.

## 8. Measurement and accountability

The first year will measure delivery, not promise predetermined ecological success.

Proposed indicators, to be finalized with independent specialists:
- Baseline data completeness and quality for the agreed pilot area.
- Permissions and stakeholder consultations completed.
- Restoration plan reviewed by qualified specialists.
- Approved interventions implemented and documented.
- Expenditure against budget, with invoice coverage.
- Monitoring observations collected using a published protocol.
- Independent review of methods and results.
- Public report distinguishing activities, outputs, outcomes, uncertainties and failures.

No hectares restored, species recovered, carbon removed, water quality improvement or biodiversity gain will be claimed before measurement and verification support the claim.

## 9. What partners receive—and what they do not buy

Partners receive:
- transparent reporting on use of their contribution;
- documented project milestones and financial reconciliation;
- optional acknowledgement as a funding or in-kind partner;
- opportunities to share technical expertise and participate in approved learning sessions;
- a credible route to support practical restoration with independent review.

Partners do not receive:
- ownership or control of NEURAL LINK;
- control over scientific methods, findings or independent verification;
- land, water, biodiversity or carbon rights;
- automatic offsets or credits;
- exclusive access to public project data or community participation;
- influence over project governance in exchange for a contribution;
- guaranteed positive publicity or environmental claims.

## 10. Priority outreach strategy

Outreach should be staged, targeted and honest. Do not send the same mass email to unrelated recipients.

### A. High-fit funding and implementation channels

1. **Deutsche Bundesstiftung Umwelt (DBU), Germany** — strongest immediate institutional route to test. DBU accepts project outlines at any time and supports practical, innovative environmental projects. Its guidance says legal entities are generally funded; individuals may be considered in individual cases. DBU generally expects applicants to provide/verify an own contribution, and typically funds a share of project costs. This means a fiscal sponsor and co-funding plan are important. [DBU project funding](https://www.dbu.de/en/promotion/project-funding/) and [DBU FAQ](https://www.dbu.de/en/promotion/project-funding/faq-on-project-funding/).

2. **Stiftung Naturschutz Schleswig-Holstein** — relevant regional nature-conservation funder. Its published eligibility includes legal entities and certain private-law persons capable of implementing eligible measures and ensuring long-term maintenance. Contact: foerderung@stiftungsland.de. [Funding information](https://www.stiftungsland.de/die-stiftung/die-stiftung-naturschutz/foerderungen/).

3. **UNEP private-sector engagement** — not a direct grant application. It offers partnership modalities with companies and alliances where participants contribute resources, expertise, technology or funding. Contact listed by UNEP private-sector engagement: unenvironment-privatesector@un.org. [Partner with UNEP](https://www.unep.org/about-un-environment/private-sector-engagement/partner-us).

4. **UNEP/GEF** — possible only if a suitable eligible country/programme and implementing partner exist. GEF resources are available to eligible recipient countries and projects; this is not a direct unrestricted personal funding route. Contact listed: unep-gef@un.org. [UNEP GEF partnership](https://www.unep.org/gef/index.php/partner-us).

### B. Strategic philanthropic prospects — approach through the correct channel

5. **Bezos Earth Fund** — strong thematic fit for nature restoration, but it explicitly says it does not accept unsolicited grant proposals or funding inquiries through its contact page. Do not send a generic funding application. Seek a relevant programme connection or introduction only. [Programmes](https://www.bezosearthfund.org/our-programs/conserving-restoring-nature) / [contact restrictions](https://www.bezosearthfund.org/contact-us).

6. **IKEA Foundation** — has substantial climate-related grantmaking but explicitly does not accept unsolicited funding requests; it selects partners through its own expert process. Use a warm introduction or an existing grantee/partner pathway, not a cold grant application. [Grantmaking approach](https://www.ikeafoundation.org/how-we-work/our-grants).

7. **Prince Albert II of Monaco Foundation** — 2026 call was limited to defined environmental priorities and priority geographies (Mediterranean, polar regions and UN least-developed countries), with shortlisted projects considered for funding from 2028. TAR-001 in Germany is not an obvious geographic match for that call. Track future calls or approach only if the project develops an eligible geographic component. [Call information](https://www.fpa2.org/en/submit-a-project).

8. **King Charles III Charitable Fund** — environment and countryside are funding themes, but small grants require a UK-registered nonprofit and large grants are invitation-only. Not a direct application route for an unregistered German initiative. Seek a qualified UK nonprofit partner only if there is a genuine UK workstream. [Grantmaking](https://www.kccf.org.uk/grantmaking/).

### C. Resource and extractive-sector partners

Approach companies with material dependence on land, water, minerals, energy, forests and agricultural supply chains. Start with sustainability, nature, foundation or community-investment teams—not royal households or generic executive inboxes.

Priority sectors:
- Mining and metals: biodiversity, water stewardship, mine-site rehabilitation and landscape restoration.
- Diamonds and gemstones: provenance, land restoration, water and community benefit.
- Oil and gas: water, biodiversity, land rehabilitation and transition funding.
- Forestry, pulp and paper: forest landscapes, soils, catchments and responsible sourcing.
- Agriculture and food: soil health, water, regenerative supply chains and landscape restoration.
- Luxury and consumer brands: material sourcing, nature-related risk and long-term supply-chain resilience.

Illustrative company prospect: **De Beers Group** publicly describes nature, biodiversity and landscape restoration partnerships as part of its Building Forever strategy. This establishes thematic relevance, not a commitment to fund NEURAL LINK. [De Beers nature programme](https://www.debeersgroup.com/sustainability/nature).

A company approach must offer a specific, independently governed contribution—not greenwashing, offsets, or the purchase of favourable claims.
