# PLANET RESTORATION FUND — ACCOUNTABILITY & FUNDING CASE v0.1
**Prepared for:** NEURAL LINK / Planet Restoration Fund  
**Date:** 18 November 2026  
**Status:** Research-based proposal; not a legal demand, invoice or adjudicated debt claim.

## 1. The proposition

The Planet Restoration Fund is proposed as a transparent mechanism to finance prevention, restoration and repair of environmental harm. It will seek contributions from high-impact producers and resource-extracting industries, public institutions, sovereign and philanthropic funds, and private donors.

The central principle is the Polluter Pays Principle: those responsible for pollution should bear the costs of preventing, controlling and remedying it. In the EU, the Environmental Liability Directive (2004/35/EC) provides a framework for specified environmental damage to protected species and habitats, water and land. It requires an identifiable operator, concrete and quantifiable damage, and a causal link; competent public authorities determine liability and remedial measures. It is not a blanket global invoice mechanism for every company in a sector.

## 2. Quantified evidence currently available

### 2.1 Oil, gas, coal and cement — climate-related damages

**Climate Analytics, 2023 — Carbon Majors’ trillion-dollar damages**
- Estimates partial climate damages attributable to emissions associated with 25 major oil and gas companies at approximately **US$20 trillion** over 1985–2018.
- Estimated financial gains over the same period were approximately **US$30 trillion**.
- The study uses a partial allocation approach to account for other contributors, including consumers and policymakers. The figures are modelled estimates, not court judgments or presently payable invoices.
- Its top-12 table identifies the following rounded partial damage estimates (2020 USD trillions): Saudi Aramco 2.8; Gazprom 2.2; National Iranian Oil Company 1.4; ExxonMobil 1.2; Pemex 1.1; Shell 1.1; BP 1.0; Chevron 0.9; PetroChina 0.9; Abu Dhabi National Oil Company 0.7; PDVSA 0.7; Kuwait Petroleum Corporation 0.6. These are estimates linked to attributed emissions, not legally established company debts.

**Nature, 23 April 2025 — Carbon majors and the scientific case for climate liability**
- A peer-reviewed perspective presents an end-to-end attribution framework linking company emissions to particular climate damages.
- For Chevron, the authors estimate emissions-linked heat losses of **US$791 billion to US$3.6 trillion** during 1991–2020. This is a research estimate of heat-related economic losses attributable through the study's method, not a judgment or a complete estimate of all environmental harm.

**Carbon Majors 2024 data update, published January 2026**
- Attributes **34.7 GtCO2e** in 2024 to 166 active oil, gas, coal and cement entities in its database.
- The 32 largest entities account for more than half of global fossil-fuel and cement CO2 emissions in 2024.
- This dataset can support updated company-by-company emissions attribution, but it does not itself convert emissions into a legally due payment.

### 2.2 Tobacco — plastic waste and marine ecosystem losses

**Tobacco Control / BMJ research, 2023**
- Estimates that plastic cigarette filters and packaging generate approximately **US$25.7 billion per year** in combined waste-management and marine ecosystem-service losses globally.
- Estimates **US$186 billion over ten years**, adjusted for inflation.
- The authors describe these as minimum estimates and a basis for assigning accountability; they do not allocate a legally payable amount to each tobacco company.
- WHO separately reports major environmental burdens from tobacco production and supply chains, including trees, land, water and CO2 impacts. Those physical indicators are not directly interchangeable with the BMJ monetary estimate.

The Fund's initial approach should demand that tobacco producers disclose product-specific plastic tonnage, filter composition, sales by market, packaging footprint and producer-responsibility payments. Company-specific amounts should only be calculated after market-share and waste-attribution data are assembled.

### 2.3 Diamond and mineral extraction

No defensible global dollar figure assigning a single debt to all diamond companies was identified in the sources reviewed. Company and mine-level assessment must use:
- mine location and operating period;
- land disturbed, habitat loss and rehabilitation status;
- water withdrawals, dewatering and contamination data;
- tailings and waste-rock volumes and treatment;
- closure and rehabilitation provisions;
- independent biodiversity baseline and post-mining monitoring;
- local legal requirements and actual remediation costs.

De Beers' 2024 sustainability reporting, for example, reports 59,114 megalitres of water withdrawn across its operations and describes biodiversity management and rehabilitation programmes. This is a company-reported operational indicator, not proof of a legal debt. Each mine must be evaluated separately.

## 3. What the Fund can legitimately demand

The Fund should issue a **Notice of Environmental Accountability and Invitation to Remedy**, not an invoice falsely claiming a court-established debt.

Each company-specific notice should contain four separate figures:
1. **Established legal liability:** amount determined by a court, regulator, settlement or binding remediation order.
2. **Verified site remediation cost:** independently assessed cost for an identified site and damage.
3. **Attributed external-cost estimate:** peer-reviewed or transparent model estimate, with period, currency, method and uncertainty range.
4. **Voluntary restoration contribution requested:** an explicitly negotiated contribution to the Fund, not represented as an existing debt.

The demand should ask each company to:
- disclose emissions, extraction, water, waste, land disturbance and rehabilitation liabilities;
- identify historic and current damage linked to its operations/products;
- provide its independent environmental liability assessment and financial provisions;
- fund prevention, remediation and independent verification;
- consider a voluntary contribution to the Planet Restoration Fund in addition to, not instead of, legal obligations;
- provide a named executive contact and response within 30 days.

The Fund must not imply that a voluntary donation settles, offsets, releases or replaces any legal liability.

- project travel and transport: €20,000;

## 6. Funding scenarios

The €2.5m target can be pursued through a diversified mix, not dependence on one donor:
- 40% corporate restoration contributions: €1,000,000;
- 30% institutional and public grants: €750,000;
- 20% philanthropic and private major donors: €500,000;
- 10% in-kind partner support: €250,000 equivalent.

These percentages are fundraising targets only. In-kind contributions cannot pay cash expenses such as card statements unless converted into a funded cash contribution under a lawful arrangement.

## 7. Governance and trust

- Independent legal entity or established fiscal sponsor must hold funds.
- Separate bank account and restricted project ledger.
- Multisignature treasury authorization; no unilateral control.
- Independent scientific verification of ecological outcomes.
- Annual financial audit and public expenditure reporting.
- Conflict-of-interest declarations and procurement rules.
- No token-holder rights over land, water, scientific conclusions or human participation.
- No claim that ZH itself represents cash, collateral, a debt claim or guaranteed redemption.
- TAR-001's existing financial gate remains inactive until its separate governance and verification requirements are met.

## 8. Legal framing

EU environmental liability law requires identifiable operators, concrete and quantifiable damage, and a causal link. The Environmental Liability Directive is implemented by national competent authorities; it is not a blanket levy automatically payable to this Fund. A private Fund can request disclosure, seek voluntary contributions, support independent research, and refer evidence to competent authorities. Any formal demand for payment must be reviewed by qualified counsel and supported by jurisdiction-specific evidence.

For global climate damages, academic attribution studies can support advocacy and potential legal claims, but their estimates must be labelled accurately and cannot be described as final debts without a legal determination.
